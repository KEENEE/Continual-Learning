
## A1. Morning Routine (Wake → Leave Home)

PATTERN TASK ID: A1
PATTERN NAME: Morning Routine (Wake → Leave Home)
TAXONOMY: Temporal Routine Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task captures the user's habitual sequence of actions from the moment they wake up (sleep record ends / first screen unlock / first app activity after a sleep period) until they depart from their home location. 
The morning routine is one of the most predictable windows in a person's day because it is time-pressured and repeated almost identically on similar day-types (weekday vs. weekend). 
Capturing this chain enables the assistant to proactively surface information (weather, commute ETA, calendar preview, news briefing) at exactly the right micro-moment.

SCOPE BOUNDARY: This task covers ONLY the wake-to-departure window. It must NOT overlap with:
  - D2 (post-wake routine chain) — D2 focuses narrowly on the first 5 minutes after waking (sleep end → screen on → first 2-3 app opens). A1 covers the ENTIRE morning arc including later actions like checking calendar, opening maps, leaving home.
  - C1 (commute departure) — C1 starts at the moment the user leaves home. A1 ends there.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXAMPLE PATTERNS YOU SHOULD LOOK FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. "Every weekday, user wakes at ~07:20, unlocks phone within 2 min, opens KakaoTalk, then checks Instagram for 5-8 min, opens Weather app, then opens Naver Map at ~07:55 right before departing home (location change detected at ~08:05)."
2. "On weekends, user wakes ~10:00-10:30, stays in bed browsing YouTube for 20-40 min before any other app, never opens Maps before noon."
3. "After waking, user always dismisses overnight notifications (KakaoTalk, news) within the first 3 minutes, then opens a finance app (Bithumb or BankSalad) before doing anything else."
4. "User checks apartment-related notification (Hogangnono real-estate app) every morning within 15 min of waking."
5. "Morning app sequence is consistently: Messaging → Finance → News → Maps → (leave home)."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DATA SOURCES TO EXAMINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIMARY:
  - [5] Sleep records: use `time` (sleep start) and `time_wakeup` to anchor wake moment.
  - [1] App Usage: all events from wake time until departure.
  - [3] Location: detect home location (the location_label where sleep occurs) and the first significant location change after wake.
  - [0] Notifications: which notifications arrived overnight and which ones the user interacts with first after waking.

SECONDARY:
  - [7] Calendar: any morning calendar events that might alter the routine.
  - [4] Movement: walking/in_vehicle detection that marks departure.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TRIGGER IDENTIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
The trigger for a morning routine is ONE of the following (pick the earliest available signal):
  1. Sleep record's `time_wakeup` field.
  2. First event after a gap of ≥3 hours of no activity (indicating overnight sleep even if no formal sleep record exists).
  3. First APP event after the sleep window.

END BOUNDARY:
  The morning routine ends at the EARLIEST of:
  a. First location change away from the home location (distance_moved > 200m from home coordinates).
  b. If the user does not leave home (weekend/holiday/remote-work), end the window at the point where the user's app-usage pattern shifts from "morning prep" apps to "leisure/work" apps — operationally, cap at wake + 2 hours if no departure is detected.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — Identify all wake events across the 4 weeks window.
  For each day, record: date, day-of-week, weekend flag, wake time, sleep duration.

STEP 2 — For each wake event, collect ALL log entries from wake_time to end_boundary (as defined above). This is the candidate morning-routine window.

STEP 3 — Normalize each morning window into an ordered action list:
  - Merge consecutive RESUMED events for the same app into a single "session" with start and duration.
  - Note notification interactions (seen=true with reason=click) and their timestamps relative to wake.
  - Note connectivity changes.
  - Note location changes.

STEP 4 — Align and compare morning windows across days.
  - **Separate weekdays and weekends**, but do not separate by day of the week. (2026-04-06 is **Monday**. Double check the day of the week from this hint.).
  - For each group, look for:
    a. Consistent app-open ORDER (even if absolute times shift, does the relative sequence stay the same?).
    b. Consistent TIME OFFSETS from wake (e.g., always opens Maps 30-40 min after waking).
    c. Consistent notification triage behavior (which notification categories get clicked first?).
    d. Consistent total duration of morning routine (wake-to-departure delta).

STEP 5 — Identify VARIANTS and CONDITIONS.
  - Does a shorter sleep duration (< user's median) change the morning routine? (e.g., skip news, go straight to Maps).
  - Does a calendar event in the morning (e.g., early meeting) compress or alter the sequence?
  - Does a specific notification (e.g., finance alert) cause the user to insert an extra app session?

STEP 6 — For each discovered sub-pattern, assemble the output object:
  - the wake or condition event (with time and context).
  - `pattern_sequence`: the ordered list of actions from trigger to end boundary.
  - `context`: include day-of-week, weekend flag, sleep_duration, home location_id, connection type.
  - `pattern_description`: abstract each action (e.g., "Open KakaoTalk", "Check Bithumb notification", "Open Naver Map", "Depart home").
  - `reasoning`: explain why this sequence is expected given the context.

STEP 7 — Validate: discard any pattern with fewer than 2 occurrences. Assign confidence:
  - high: appears on ≥70% of applicable days with consistent sequence.
  - medium: appears on 40-69% of applicable days or with minor variations.
  - low: appears on 2-3 days only, or with significant variation.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- Focus your description and prediction on the FULL morning flow, not just the first 2-3 minutes. D2 will zoom into the micro-chain.
- If the user works from home on certain days (no location change detected), still extract the morning routine up to the 2-hour cap — these "no-commute mornings" are a valuable variant.
- Pay attention to notification dismissal vs. app usage patterns in the morning — this reveals priority/triage behavior unique to this user.




## A2. Midday / Lunch Routine

```
PATTERN TASK ID: A2

PATTERN NAME: Midday / Lunch Routine
TAXONOMY: Temporal Routine Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task captures the user's recurring behavior during the midday period, roughly 11:00–14:00. 
The lunch routine often involves a location change (leaving workplace to a restaurant or café), specific app usage (food delivery apps, restaurant search, payment apps), 
communication bursts (coordinating lunch with colleagues), and post-lunch behavior (coffee order, short walk, brief social media check). 
Identifying this pattern enables the assistant to suggest restaurants, pre-fill delivery orders, or remind the user of afternoon calendar events as lunch ends.

SCOPE BOUNDARY: This task covers the 11:00–14:00 window broadly. It must NOT overlap with:
  - A8 (weekday working hours) — A8 covers generic work-hour behavior EXCLUDING the lunch break. A2 specifically targets the lunch-break interruption.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXAMPLE PATTERNS YOU SHOULD LOOK FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. "Every weekday around 12:00-12:15, user opens a food delivery app (Baemin/Yogiyo), places an order, then switches to YouTube for 15-20 min while waiting."
2. "User leaves workplace location at ~12:30, moves to a nearby restaurant cluster, returns at ~13:20, then opens a café app (Starbucks) for a coffee order."
3. "Lunch break always starts with a group chat burst (3-5 messages in the 'team lunch' chat), followed by location departure within 10 min."
4. "On days the user skips lunch out (no location change 11:00-14:00), they order delivery and browse Instagram for 20+ min — different from days they eat out."
5. "Post-lunch, user consistently opens a news app or finance app between 13:00-13:30 before resuming work apps."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DATA SOURCES TO EXAMINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIMARY:
  - [1] App Usage: all events in the 11:00–14:00 window. Look for food/delivery/restaurant/payment apps.
  - [3] Location: any departure from and return to the workplace(Location 1) or any primary location .
  - [4] Movement: walking activity around 12:00-13:00 (walking to restaurant).
  - [0] Notifications: lunch-coordination messages, delivery status notifications, payment confirmations.
  - [6] Calls: short calls around noon (coordinating lunch).
  - [7] Calendar: lunch meetings or blocked lunch slots.

SECONDARY:
  - [2] Network: Wifi disconnect/reconnect at workplace (signals leaving and returning).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TRIGGER IDENTIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Possible triggers for the lunch routine:
  1. TIME-BASED: The clock hitting a consistent time (e.g., 12:00 ± 15 min) on weekdays.
  2. APP-BASED: Opening a food-related app or messaging app with lunch-coordination content.
  3. LOCATION-BASED: Departure from home/workplace location during the 11:00-14:00 window.
  4. NOTIFICATION-BASED: A message in a group chat that historically precedes lunch departure.
  5. CALENDAR-BASED: A "lunch" or "식사" calendar event.

END BOUNDARY:
  The lunch routine ends when:
  a. User returns to workplace location in weekdays or home location in weekend OR
  b. User resumes work-related app usage (if remote/WFH), OR
  c. 14:00, whichever comes first.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — For each of the 28 days, extract all log entries between 11:00 and 14:00.

STEP 2 — Classify each day's midday window:
  a. "Eat-out day": location change detected away from primary daytime location.
  b. "Delivery day": food delivery app opened but no location change.
  c. "Skip/unclear": no food-related signal detected.
  d. "Weekend/holiday": different baseline expected.

STEP 3 — For each category, align the action sequences:
  - What app or event marks the START of lunch behavior? (First non-work app? First food app? First location departure?)
  - What is the sequence of apps/actions during lunch?
  - What marks the END of lunch? (Return to workplace? Resume of work app?)
  - How long is the lunch break (departure-to-return or first-food-app to first-work-app)?

STEP 4 — Look for consistent sub-patterns:
  a. Pre-lunch coordination: Does the user always message someone before lunch? Same group chat?
  b. During-lunch media: Does the user watch YouTube or browse social media while eating?
  c. Post-lunch ritual: Coffee order? Specific app? Short walk?
  d. Lunch timing drift: Does lunch start later on certain days (e.g., meeting-heavy days)?

STEP 5 — Check for CONDITIONAL variants:
  - Does weather or day-of-week affect eat-out vs. delivery choice?
  - Does a morning calendar event (e.g., meeting ending at 12:30) shift lunch timing?
  - On weekends, is there a different lunch pattern (later, longer, different apps)?

STEP 6 — Assemble output objects for each discovered pattern. Each occurrence should include:
  - the event that initiates lunch behavior.
  - `pattern_sequence`: full lunch action chain.
  - `pattern_description`: abstracted next actions (e.g., "Open Baemin delivery app", "Depart to restaurant area", "Open YouTube", "Return to office", "Order coffee on Starbucks app").
  - `context`: day-of-week, weekend flag, location_id, any relevant calendar event.
  - `reasoning`: explain why this sequence is expected given the context.

STEP 7 — Validate ≥2 occurrences. Assign confidence based on consistency across applicable days.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- On weekends and holidays, the user may not have a structured lunch routine. If a weekend lunch pattern exists, report it separately with weekend=true context.
- Food delivery app names in Korea include 배달의민족 (Baemin), 요기요 (Yogiyo), 쿠팡이츠 (Coupang Eats). Also look for 카카오톡 order features.
- Payment notifications (삼성카드, etc.) around lunchtime can confirm a purchase even if no food app was opened.
```

## A3. Afternoon Routine

```
PATTERN TASK ID: A3
PATTERN NAME: Afternoon Routine
TAXONOMY: Temporal Routine Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task captures recurring user behavior during the afternoon window, approximately 14:00–17:30 on weekdays and 13:00–17:30 on weekends. 
The afternoon is often the least structured part of the day, but users still exhibit micro-routines: 
a post-lunch energy dip leading to specific app usage (social media, news), a mid-afternoon coffee break, 
checking financial markets before close (weekday 15:30 KST for Korean stock market), or a pre-departure routine as the workday winds down. 

SCOPE BOUNDARY:
  - Starts around 13:00, ends where A4 (dinner) begins (~17:30) or C2 (commute home) begins, whichever is earlier.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXAMPLE PATTERNS YOU SHOULD LOOK FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. "Every weekday around 14:30-15:00, user opens Instagram or Twitter for a 5-10 min break, then returns to work apps."
2. "User checks stock/crypto app (Bithumb) around 15:00-15:30 (Korean market close) on weekdays."
3. "Around 16:00, user opens a café/coffee app to order afternoon coffee — this happens 4 out of 5 weekdays."
4. "On weekends, user leaves home around 14:00-15:00 for errands (location changes to shopping areas)."
5. "User starts winding down work at ~17:00: switches from work apps to messaging apps, checks evening plans in calendar."
6. "On Fridays specifically, user opens a restaurant reservation app around 16:00 (planning weekend dining)."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DATA SOURCES TO EXAMINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIMARY:
  - [1] App Usage: all events 13:00–17:30. Identify work vs. non-work app clusters.
  - [0] Notifications: which notifications get clicked vs. dismissed in this window.
  - [3] Location: any mid-afternoon location changes (errands, coffee run, gym).

SECONDARY:
  - [7] Calendar: afternoon meetings or events that structure the window.
  - [2] Network: connectivity changes suggesting movement.
  - [4] Movement: walking/vehicle activity mid-afternoon.
  - [6] Calls: afternoon call patterns.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TRIGGER IDENTIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Afternoon micro-routines may be triggered by one of these.
  1. Return to office(Location1)
  2. specific clock times (e.g., 15:00 market close, 16:00 coffee break).
  3. NOTIFICATION: a specific recurring notification (e.g., stock alert, delivery arrival).
  4. DURATION: after N hours of continuous work-app usage, user takes a break.

END BOUNDARY:
  - First signal of commute-home preparation (opening Maps, location departure from workplace), OR
  - 17:30 if no departure signal.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — For each day, extract all events in the 14:00–17:30 window. Tag each day as weekday/weekend/holiday.

STEP 2 — Identify "break" micro-sessions: any non-work app usage lasting >1 min surrounded by work-app usage. Log the break app, start time, duration, and what preceded/followed it.

STEP 3 — Look for time-anchored habits:
  - Bin events into 30-min slots (14:00, 14:30, 15:00, ..., 17:00).
  - For each slot, count which apps appear across multiple days. If an app appears in the same slot on ≥2 days, flag it as a candidate afternoon habit.

STEP 4 — Look for sequence patterns:
  - What is the common behavior the user do afternoon?
  - Does a sleep duration change the pattern?
  - Is there a consistent "wind-down" sequence in the last 30 min before departure?

STEP 5 — Weekend afternoon: separately analyze weekend days.
  - Does the user leave home for errands? What apps are used before departure (shopping list, maps)?
  - Is there a consistent weekend afternoon leisure pattern (cafe, gaming, reading)?

STEP 6 — Assemble output objects. For each pattern:
  - Clearly distinguish weekday vs. weekend occurrences.
  - In `context`, note if a calendar event preceded the pattern.
  - In `prediction`, be specific: "Open Bithumb to check crypto prices", not "Check finance app".

STEP 7 — Validate ≥2 occurrences. Assign confidence.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- The afternoon is often less patterned than morning or evening. Look carefully for micro-breaks and time-anchored checks (finance, social media).
- Korean stock market closes at 15:30 KST — if the user trades or monitors stocks, this is a strong time anchor.
- Distinguish between "afternoon routine on a normal workday" vs. "afternoon on a irregular day" if unique data discovered.
```

## A4. Dinner Time Routine

```
PATTERN TASK ID: A4
PATTERN NAME: Dinner Time Routine
TAXONOMY: Temporal Routine Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task captures the user's recurring behavior around dinner time, approximately 17:30–20:00. 
This window includes dinner preparation or ordering, mealtime media consumption, and post-dinner transition into evening leisure. 
The dinner routine is valuable for suggesting recipes or restaurants, timing smart-home actions (lights, music), and knowing when the user transitions from "productive" to "relaxation" mode.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXAMPLE PATTERNS YOU SHOULD LOOK FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. "User calls a family member (parent) between 18:00-19:00 on weekdays while commuting or right after arriving home."
2. "During dinner (19:00-20:00), user watches YouTube or Netflix — the longest continuous media session of the day."
3. "On Fridays and Saturdays, user goes to a restaurant (location change to dining area) instead of ordering delivery."
4. "Post-dinner, user always opens a messaging app (KakaoTalk) for 5-10 min, then transitions to Instagram."
5. "User orders coffee/dessert around 19:30-20:00 after finishing dinner — detected via payment notification or café app."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DATA SOURCES TO EXAMINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIMARY:
  - [1] App Usage: food apps, media apps, messaging apps in the 17:30–20:30 window.
  - [3] Location: arrival at home, or departure to restaurant.
  - [0] Notifications: payment confirmations, dinner-coordination messages.
  - [4] Movement: transition with walking or in_vehicle.

SECONDARY:
  - [6] Calls: dinner-time calls (family, friends).
  - [7] Calendar: dinner plans, social events.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TRIGGER IDENTIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  1. Location change (strongest trigger for dinner routine).
  2. TIME: consistent dinner-start time (e.g., 18:30 ± 20 min).
  3. APP: opening a food app or recipe app.
  4. CALL: calling may signal user's dinner plan or call routine.

END BOUNDARY:
  - Transition to non-food, non-meal-related leisure (e.g., sustained social media or entertainment app usage without food-app interspersion).
  - Operationally: ~20:00–20:30 or when the user's app pattern shifts to evening-leisure mode.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — For each day, identify the dinner window:
  - Find home-arrival time (location + network change) or, if user was already home, use 17:30 as start.
  - Collect all events from dinner-start to 20:30.

STEP 2 — Classify dinner type for each day:
  a. "Eat-out": location change to a non-home, non-work location during 17:30-20:30.
  b. "Cook-at-home": no food app, no location change (may see recipe app or no food signal at all).
  c. "Social dinner": calendar event or group chat coordination + eat-out.

STEP 3 — For each dinner type, extract the action sequence:
  - Pre-dinner: what happens in the 15 min before food-related activity? (Commute arrival? Changing clothes = no phone activity for 5 min?)
  - During dinner: silent or media consumption? (which app, how long).
  - Post-dinner: first app opened after the meal-media session ends.

STEP 4 — Look for day-of-week variations:
  - Weekday dinner vs. weekend dinner.
  - Friday dinner (often social/eat-out).
  - Specific day patterns (e.g., every Wednesday = late work, dinner after 20:00).

STEP 5 — Look for communication patterns around dinner:
  - Does the user call parents/partner at a consistent time?
  - Does a KakaoTalk message trigger a unique dinner pattern?

STEP 6 — Assemble output objects with full context. Include dinner-type classification in context.

STEP 7 — Validate ≥2 occurrences. Assign confidence.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- Dinner is highly variable by day-of-week. Separate weekday and weekend patterns.
- Payment notifications in this window are strong signals of food purchase even without a food app open. No payment also signals meaningful dinner pattern.
- If the user lives alone vs. with family, dinner patterns differ significantly. Infer from data: if calls to "아빠" (dad) or "엄마" (mom) happen from home location, user likely lives alone and calls family. If no such calls and no delivery apps, user may eat with household members.
```

```
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  1. TIME: the evening block starts at a relatively consistent time after dinner.
  2. APP TRANSITION: switching from dinner-associated apps to evening-leisure apps.
  3. CONTEXT: move to workplace / home / 3rd places

END BOUNDARY:
  - The start of D1 (pre-sleep routine): typically marked by the user's last "active" app session before a prolonged screen-off or KEYGUARD_SHOWN that leads to sleep.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

STEP 2 — Compute app-session summaries:
  - For each app, calculate total foreground time in the evening window.
  - Rank apps by total evening usage across all 28 days. The top 5 are the user's "evening staple apps."

STEP 3 — Extract app-transition sequences:
  - List the order of app sessions each evening (ignoring sub-30-second app opens as accidental/transitional).
  - Align sequences across days. Look for consistent ordering (e.g., YouTube → Instagram → Hogangnono → FLO).

STEP 4 — Identify time-anchored evening habits:
  - Bin into 30-min slots. For each slot, which app dominates across multiple days?
  - Is there a consistent "phase transition" time (e.g., 22:00 = switch from video to social media)?

STEP 5 — Look for notification-driven evening behavior:
  - Which notification types get clicked in the evening? (Social notifications → open KakaoTalk? Deal alerts → open shopping app?)
  - Does a specific notification reliably trigger a 10+ min app session?

STEP 6 — Weekend vs. weekday evening:
  - Do weekday evenings end earlier (user sleeps earlier for work)?
  - Are weekend evenings longer with different app mix (more gaming, shopping, social)?

STEP 7 — Assemble output objects. In `prediction`, capture the evening flow:
  e.g., "Watch YouTube for ~60 min" → "Browse Instagram for ~20 min" → "Check Bithumb crypto prices" → "Listen to FLO music" → "Begin pre-sleep wind-down."

STEP 8 — Validate ≥2 occurrences. Assign confidence.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- Evening routines are often the most consistent and longest patterns. Prioritize capturing the dominant app sequence.
- Music apps (FLO) running in background while other apps are in foreground is common — detect this via FOREGROUND_SERVICE_START for music apps concurrent with other app ACTIVITY_RESUMED events.
- The sample data shows FLO and YouTube used around 00:47 — this may be an evening-to-late-night transition. Check if this is typical.
```

## A6. Late-Night / After-Midnight Pattern

```
PATTERN TASK ID: A6
PATTERN NAME: Late-Night / After-Midnight Pattern
TAXONOMY: Temporal Routine Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task captures user behavior occurring after 22:00 and before sleep onset. 
Many users have a distinct late-night behavioral mode that differs from their regular evening routine: more impulsive browsing, late-night shopping, doom-scrolling social media, checking crypto/stock prices during off-hours markets, or consuming content they wouldn't during the day. 
Late-night patterns are especially important because they directly impact sleep quality and next-day behavior (see D3, D4).
```

```
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — Extract all sleep records and tag each by the night's day-of-week:
  - Sleep starting Sunday night = "Sunday night" (before Monday).
  - Sleep starting Monday night = "Monday night" (before Tuesday).
  - Continue through Thursday night (before Friday).

STEP 2 — For weeknight sleep records, compute:
  - Mean and standard deviation of sleep onset time.
  - Mean and standard deviation of wake time.
  - Mean and standard deviation of sleep duration.
  - Min and max values for each.

STEP 3 — Check for DAY-SPECIFIC variations within weekdays:
  - Is Sunday night consistently later (weekend-to-weekday transition)?
  - Is Thursday night consistently later (pre-Friday relaxation)?
  - Is any specific weeknight an outlier?

STEP 4 — Check for TRENDS over the 4 weeks period:
  - Is bedtime getting later or earlier over time?
  - Is sleep duration increasing or decreasing?
  - Is there a "sleep debt accumulation" pattern (progressively shorter sleep Mon→Thu, then recovery on weekend)?

STEP 5 — Correlate with next-day events:
  - Does an early calendar event (before 09:00) correlate with earlier bedtime or earlier wake time?
  - Does a late calendar event (after 10:00) correlate with later bedtime?

STEP 6 — Assemble output objects. The "pattern" here is the weekday sleep baseline itself:
  - `trigger`: "It's a weeknight" or "It's [TIME] on a weeknight."
  - `prediction`: "User will fall asleep at ~[TIME], wake at ~[TIME], sleep ~[DURATION]."
  - Include variance estimates so the assistant knows the confidence range.

STEP 7 — Validate: need ≥2 weeknights (should have ~15 in 28 days). Assign confidence based on consistency (low σ = high confidence).

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- The sample data shows sleep on what appears to be a Friday night (April 18→19, Friday→Saturday): sleep at 03:32, wake at 10:24, duration 6h52m. This is a WEEKEND night, not a weeknight. The weekday sleep pattern may be very different.
- If the user has irregular work hours (shift work, freelance), weekday vs. weekend distinction may not apply. Detect this from the data.
- Sleep "confidence" from the sleep record (high/medium/low) indicates how reliable the sleep detection is. Weight high-confidence records more heavily.
```

## D6. Weekend Sleep Routine

```
PATTERN TASK ID: D6
PATTERN NAME: Weekend Sleep Routine
TAXONOMY: Sleep & Health Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task characterizes the user's typical sleep pattern on weekend nights (Friday and Saturday nights) and compares it with the weekday baseline (D5). Weekend sleep often differs significantly: later bedtime, later wake time, longer duration (catch-up sleep), no alarm, and different pre-sleep behavior. Understanding the weekend sleep pattern helps the assistant adjust its behavior on weekends (no early morning alarm suggestion, later morning briefing, different bedtime reminder timing).

SCOPE BOUNDARY:
  - Covers Friday night and Saturday night sleep periods.
  - Also covers holiday-eve nights (night before a public holiday) if they show weekend-like sleep patterns.
  - Focuses on SLEEP METRICS and COMPARISON WITH WEEKDAY BASELINE.
  - Must NOT overlap with D5 (weekday sleep).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXAMPLE PATTERNS YOU SHOULD LOOK FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. "Weekend bedtime: 02:00 ± 45 min (vs. weekday 00:30). Wake time: 10:00 ± 30 min (vs. weekday 07:15). Duration: 8h00m (vs. weekday 6h45m)."
2. "User sleeps 1h15m longer on weekends — compensating for weekday sleep debt."
3. "Friday night bedtime is later than Saturday night (Friday = social night, Saturday = recovery)."
4. "User does NOT use an alarm on weekend mornings (no Clock app activity at wake time)."
5. "Weekend sleep onset is more variable (σ = 45 min) than weekday (σ = 20 min) — less structured."
6. "On holiday eves (like Thursday night before 4·19 holiday), user sleeps on a weekend schedule."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DATA SOURCES TO EXAMINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIMARY:
  - [5] Sleep: all sleep records for Friday and Saturday nights.
  - D5 data: weekday baseline for comparison.

SECONDARY:
  - [7] Calendar: holidays, next-day events.
  - [1] App Usage: alarm app absence on weekend mornings.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TRIGGER IDENTIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  1. WEEKEND NIGHT CONTEXT: it's Friday or Saturday night → weekend sleep routine applies.
  2. HOLIDAY EVE: tomorrow is a holiday → weekend sleep routine may apply even on a weeknight.
  3. NO NEXT-DAY OBLIGATIONS: calendar is empty tomorrow morning → user may adopt weekend sleep pattern.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — Extract all sleep records for Friday and Saturday nights.

STEP 2 — Compute weekend sleep statistics:
  - Mean and standard deviation of sleep onset, wake time, duration.
  - Compare with weekday baseline (D5).

STEP 3 — Compute the WEEKDAY-WEEKEND DELTA:
  - Bedtime shift: how much later does the user go to bed on weekends?
  - Wake time shift: how much later does the user wake on weekends?
  - Duration change: how much longer does the user sleep on weekends?
  - This delta is a key metric — it indicates the user's "social jet lag" (misalignment between weekday and weekend sleep schedules).

STEP 4 — Check Friday vs. Saturday night differences:
  - Friday night is often the latest (social activities, no next-day pressure).
  - Saturday night may be earlier (preparing for Sunday activities, or simply recovered from Friday).

STEP 5 — Check holiday-eve nights:
  - Does the user sleep on a weekend schedule the night before a holiday?
  - In the sample: April 18 (Friday) night → April 19 (Saturday, also 4·19 holiday). Sleep at 03:32, wake at 10:24. This is a Friday night / holiday eve — likely weekend pattern.

STEP 6 — Assemble output objects. The key output is the weekend sleep profile and its delta from weekday:
  - `trigger`: "It's a weekend night" or "Tomorrow is a holiday."
  - `prediction`: "User will fall asleep at ~[TIME], wake at ~[TIME], sleep ~[DURATION]."
  - `reasoning`: "Weekend sleep is typically [DELTA] later/longer than weekday."

STEP 7 — Validate: need ≥2 weekend nights (should have ~6 in 28 days: 3 Fridays + 3 Saturdays). Assign confidence.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- The sample data provides one weekend sleep data point: Friday night, sleep at 03:32, wake at 10:24, 6h52m. This is late but not exceptionally long — the user may have stayed up late but still didn't sleep in much (perhaps due to the holiday plans).
- "Social jet lag" (large weekday-weekend sleep timing difference) is associated with health risks. If detected, the assistant could gently suggest more consistent sleep timing.
- If the user's weekend and weekday sleep are similar (low delta), that's also a pattern worth reporting — it indicates a consistent sleeper.
```

## D7. Nap Patterns

```
PATTERN TASK ID: D7
PATTERN NAME: Nap Patterns
TAXONOMY: Sleep & Health Patterns

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESCRIPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This task detects daytime naps — short sleep periods occurring outside the main nighttime sleep window. Naps can be detected through: (1) explicit daytime sleep records from the sleep sensor, (2) extended phone-idle periods during the day (no app activity for 20-60 min while at home/office), or (3) specific nap-related app usage (alarm set for 20-30 min, sleep tracking app activated during the day). Understanding nap patterns helps the assistant: predict afternoon drowsiness, suggest optimal nap timing, avoid interrupting naps with notifications, and correlate naps with nighttime sleep quality.

SCOPE BOUNDARY:
  - Covers any sleep or sleep-like period occurring between 10:00 and 20:00 (daytime).
  - Must NOT overlap with D5/D6 (nighttime sleep routines) — D7 is daytime only.
  - Must NOT overlap with D3 (short-sleep ripple effects) — D3 covers the behavioral changes on short-sleep days; D7 specifically captures the NAP itself as a compensating action.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXAMPLE PATTERNS YOU SHOULD LOOK FOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. "User takes a 20-30 min nap on weekday afternoons (14:00-15:00) — detected by phone-idle period at workplace."
2. "User naps on weekends around 14:00-16:00 — detected by daytime sleep record."
3. "Naps occur more frequently on days following short nighttime sleep (< 6 hours) — compensatory napping."
4. "User sets a 25-min alarm before napping (Clock app activity followed by phone-idle period)."
5. "After napping, user's first action is checking KakaoTalk (catching up on missed messages during nap)."
6. "User naps only at home, never at workplace — weekend napper."
7. "Nap duration is consistent: 20-30 min (power nap) or 60-90 min (full sleep cycle)."

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DATA SOURCES TO EXAMINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PRIMARY:
  - [5] Sleep: any sleep records with onset time between 10:00 and 20:00.
  - [1] App Usage: phone-idle periods (gaps of 20+ min with no ACTIVITY_RESUMED events) during daytime while at home.
  - KEYGUARD events: screen-off periods during daytime.

SECONDARY:
  - [3] Location: nap location (home vs. workplace vs. other).
  - [0] Notifications: notifications that accumulated during the nap (dismissed in bulk after waking = nap confirmation).
  - [5] Sleep (previous night): short nighttime sleep → daytime nap correlation.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
TRIGGER IDENTIFICATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  1. TIME: consistent nap time (e.g., always 14:00-15:00).
  2. SHORT PRIOR NIGHT SLEEP: sleep duration < threshold → nap likely today.
  3. POST-LUNCH: after lunch (A2 ends) → drowsiness → nap.
  4. WEEKEND + AFTERNOON: weekend afternoon at home → nap likely.
  5. ALARM SET: user sets a short alarm (20-30 min) during daytime → nap intent.

END BOUNDARY:
  - Nap ends when: sleep record ends, or phone activity resumes after the idle period.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTRACTION PROCEDURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP 1 — Search for EXPLICIT nap records:
  - Any sleep record with onset time between 10:00 and 20:00.
  - These are the most reliable nap detections.

STEP 2 — Search for IMPLICIT nap signals:
  - Phone-idle periods of 20-90 min during daytime (no app activity, screen off).
  - Filter out: work-on-computer periods (these also show phone idle but are not naps). Distinguish by:
    - Location: idle at home on a weekend = likely nap. Idle at workplace on a weekday = likely working on computer.
    - Time: idle at 14:00-16:00 = more likely nap than idle at 10:00-12:00.
    - Post-idle behavior: after a nap, user often checks notifications in bulk. After computer work, user may open a work app.

STEP 3 — For each detected nap:
  - Record: date, day-of-week, start time, duration, location.
  - Record: previous night's sleep duration (to check for compensatory napping).
  - Record: pre-nap behavior (last app before nap, any alarm set).
  - Record: post-nap behavior (first app after nap, notification triage).

STEP 4 — Compute nap statistics:
  - Frequency: how many naps per week?
  - Timing: typical nap time?
  - Duration: typical nap length?
  - Day-of-week: more naps on weekends? Specific weekdays?

STEP 5 — Correlate naps with nighttime sleep:
  - Do naps follow short-sleep nights?
  - Do naps affect the following night's sleep (later bedtime, shorter duration)?

STEP 6 — Assemble output objects. The `trigger` may be:
  - "It's 14:00 on a weekend after a short-sleep night" → predict nap.
  - "User set a 25-min alarm at 14:30" → predict nap.

STEP 7 — Validate ≥2 nap occurrences. Assign confidence.

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- Naps are often ABSENT from the data — many users don't nap regularly. If no naps are detected, report that finding ("User does not appear to nap").
- The sample data does not show a daytime nap (the sleep record is nighttime: 03:32-10:24). Check the full 4 weeks dataset.
- Be careful not to confuse phone-idle periods during work (user is on computer) with naps. Use location and time-of-day to disambiguate.
- Short naps (< 20 min) may not be detected by sleep sensors. Phone-idle periods are the backup detection method.
- If the user naps with music playing (FLO FOREGROUND_SERVICE active during phone-idle period), this is a nap signal, not a "user is awake listening to music" signal.
```
